Showroom Location Selection in Ahmedabad

The best showroom location is the one that converts the right customer while supporting operations and sustainable occupancy cost. This guide helps brands move beyond traffic counts and evaluate the complete journey, premises capability, approvals and financial case.

By PIKORUA Realty Research Desk · Updated 12 September 2026

Define the visit mission

Identify whether customers browse, collect, consult, test, configure or complete high-value appointments. Estimate party size, dwell time, peak periods and repeat frequency.

The mission determines visibility, floor plate, room mix, parking, loading and the value of co-tenants.

Map catchment and competition

Use customer addresses, demographics, sales data, competitor observation and drive-route analysis. Distinguish the people who pass from those likely to visit.

Visit competing locations at different times and document access, parking, experience and positioning rather than copying their road choice.

Audit the approach and frontage

Drive every relevant direction, record turn movements, service-road entry, sightline and safe exit. Walk the entrance and accessible route.

Measure frontage and verify signage permissions. Digital mock-ups do not prove real-world visibility or approval.

Confirm premises suitability

Check use, approvals, fire safety, power, HVAC, structure, floor loading, water, drainage, exhaust, loading, storage, waste, hours and fit-out constraints.

Resolve material gaps and responsibilities before signing because later changes can affect opening cost and schedule.

Build the investment case

Model rent and all occupancy charges, fit-out, signage, technology, staffing, logistics, marketing and ramp-up against conservative sales scenarios.

For an SG Highway or Million Minds Tech City opportunity, request exact unit and phase evidence; broad corridor or master-plan language is not availability.

Questions answered

Frequently asked questions

Is the busiest road always the best showroom location?

No. Relevant customer intent, safe access, visibility, parking and conversion matter more than traffic volume alone.

How much frontage is enough?

It depends on format and signage; measure usable frontage and test sightlines from real approaches.

What should be confirmed before signing?

Use, approvals, services, signage, access, parking, loading, handover, costs and fit-out responsibilities.

How should future development be treated?

As a dated scenario with evidence and dependencies, not as existing footfall.

Can an office-led development support retail?

Potentially, if the format matches actual occupier demand, access, operating hours and approved premises.
