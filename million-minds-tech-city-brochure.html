Where to get the brochure

Use the official project-domain PDF rather than an unverified file shared through a portal or message. The publisher may revise or replace the PDF at the same URL, so save the access date and ask the leasing contact for the current revision when discussing a particular tower, floor or unit.

This independent site does not rehost the developer's brochure or claim ownership of its drawings. The link takes you to the publisher's document; our notes are an interpretation for due diligence, not a substitute for the original material.

What the M One pages show

The brochure describes M One Phase 1 and includes a ground-floor arrival plan, a first-floor podium plan and grouped office-floor layouts. It identifies vehicle and pedestrian entries, visitor areas, lift lobbies, service circulation, amenity areas, restrooms and fire stairs. The office drawings distinguish different floor groups and mark office areas, corridors and refuge spaces.

A headline floor plate is not a unit's contractual area. In the brochure, some illustrated floor-group area labels differ and the notes distinguish gross carpet and refuge components. Ask for a dimensioned, current plan for the exact premises and have an architect test the seating, meeting, support, circulation and compliance programme against it.

Specifications and amenities to check

The brochure refers to a typical approximately 42,000 sq. ft. carpet floor plate, a 4.2-metre typical floor height, power backup, centralised cooling, lifts, building-management systems, EV parking and fire-safety systems. It also illustrates lifestyle, food, childcare and fitness amenities.

Treat each item as a published design or marketing statement until the current delivery stage and exact scope are verified. Request a technical data sheet, utility capacities, equipment schedules, maintenance responsibilities and evidence of any certification relied on by your organisation.

Brochure versus transaction documents

A brochure helps build a shortlist; it is not a live availability register, signed rent quote, approved fit-out plan or proof of occupancy permission. A commercial proposal should state area definitions, premises identity, base rent, maintenance, deposits, escalation, fit-out allowance, handover condition, taxes and key dates in writing.

If SEZ treatment matters to the occupier, request the applicable approvals and professional advice for the particular premises and entity. Do not infer eligibility from a brochure headline alone. The separate SEZ guide sets out the questions to take to legal and tax teams.

A practical document request list

Before a site visit, request the current brochure revision, master plan, exact tower and floor plan, area statement, technical specifications, amenities schedule and status of the spaces being offered. After the visit, ask for the marked-up premises plan, service-level information, fit-out rules and a dated proposal.

Compare every figure across documents. If the brochure, plan and offer letter disagree, ask the owner or authorised leasing representative to reconcile them in writing rather than selecting the most favourable number. Keep the final approved set with the lease record.
